Module 2: Practical AI Tools

Techniques for getting genuinely useful results from AI — especially for school.

Prompting: give the AI a good brief

A prompt is an instruction, and vague instructions get vague results. Strong prompts include:

  • Role: "Act as a patient geometry tutor…"
  • Context: "I'm in 10th grade and we're studying quadratic equations…"
  • Format: "Explain in 3 bullet points, then give one practice problem."
  • Constraints: "Don't just give the answer — walk me through the steps."

Tools worth knowing

  • Chatbots / LLMs — brainstorming, explaining hard concepts, drafting outlines.
  • AI search & research assistants — summarizing sources with citations you can check.
  • Code assistants — autocompleting code, explaining errors, generating boilerplate.
  • Image & audio generators — visuals for presentations and creative projects.
  • Study helpers — turning your notes into flashcards or practice quizzes.

Using AI for schoolwork — the smart way

Use AI as a tutor, not a ghostwriter. Good patterns: ask it to quiz you, explain a concept three different ways, critique your draft essay, or generate practice problems. Bad pattern: pasting in the assignment and submitting the output. The first builds understanding; the second builds nothing — and most schools now treat it as academic dishonesty.

Verify everything important

AI tools hallucinate: fake citations, wrong dates, plausible-but-false facts. Treat AI output as a first draft: cross-check facts against reliable sources, and never cite something you haven't verified yourself.
