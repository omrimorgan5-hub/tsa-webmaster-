Module 1: AI Fundamentals

What artificial intelligence actually is — and what it isn't.

What is AI?

Artificial intelligence is software that performs tasks which normally require human intelligence: recognizing images, understanding language, or making predictions. A useful way to think about it: AI finds patterns in data and uses them to make decisions about new data. It doesn't "understand" the way you do — it computes probabilities.

Machine learning: learning from examples

Instead of a programmer writing explicit rules ("if the email contains the word 'lottery', mark it spam"), machine learning feeds the computer thousands of examples and lets it discover the rules itself. The three main flavors:

  • Supervised learning — learns from labeled examples (photos tagged "cat" or "dog").
  • Unsupervised learning — finds hidden groups in unlabeled data (customer segments).
  • Reinforcement learning — learns by trial and error with rewards (game-playing AI).

Neural networks & deep learning

A neural network is layers of simple math units ("neurons") that transform inputs into outputs. Stack many layers and you get deep learning — the technique behind image recognition, voice assistants, and today's chatbots. Training means adjusting millions of tiny knobs (weights) until the network's guesses match the training data.

Generative AI & large language models

Large language models (LLMs) like the chatbots you've used are trained to predict the next word in a sequence, over trillions of words. That simple objective, at massive scale, produces surprisingly capable behavior: writing, summarizing, coding, translating. But prediction is not the same as truth — LLMs can confidently produce wrong answers ("hallucinations"), which is why verifying matters.
