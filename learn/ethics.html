Module 3: Ethical AI Use

Powerful tools come with real responsibilities. Here's how to use AI well.

Bias in, bias out

AI learns from human-created data, so it can absorb human biases. Hiring tools have downgraded résumés with women's names; image generators have stereotyped professions by gender and race. The takeaway: AI output reflects its training data — question it, especially when it affects people.

Privacy: what you type can be kept

Anything you paste into an AI tool — personal details, photos, school documents — may be stored and even used for training. Rule of thumb: never share anything with an AI chatbot that you wouldn't post publicly, and check the tool's privacy settings.

Academic integrity

Submitting AI-written work as your own is plagiarism at most schools, and AI detectors (imperfect as they are) are widely used. Beyond the rules, there's a practical cost: outsourcing your thinking means skipping the learning. Use AI to understand more deeply, not to bypass the work.

Misinformation & deepfakes

AI can generate convincing fake images, audio, and video. Before sharing something surprising: check the source, look for corroboration from trusted outlets, and be extra skeptical of emotional content — outrage is the easiest thing to manufacture. When you create AI content yourself, label it as AI-generated.
